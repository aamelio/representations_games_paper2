\section{Method}

Each participant-frame provides four HP texts, one for each allocation anchor $a\in\{4,6,8,12\}$. Each text is classified as Moral (M), Self-interest (S), Cooperation (C), or No clear justification (N). We estimate the partially pooled multinomial model

\begin{equation}
    \log\frac{\Pr(K_{ia}=k)}{\Pr(K_{ia}=N)}=\alpha_{ka}+b_{ki},
    \qquad k\in\{M,S,C\},
\end{equation}

where the participant-frame effects $b_{ki}$ are Gaussian-shrunk and the penalty is selected by cross-validation. The four weights are the fitted probabilities averaged across allocation anchors,

\begin{equation}
    w_{ik}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_a \widehat{\Pr}(K_{ia}=k).
\end{equation}

We regress the participant's DG share sent on $w_{iM}$, $w_{iS}$, and $w_{iC}$, with N omitted, controlling for game-by-condition cells. Standard errors are clustered by participant. Confidence intervals refit both stages in 500 participant-cluster bootstrap samples, holding the penalty at its cross-validated value.

A 10-percentage-point increase in the Moral weight predicts a 1.26-percentage-point increase in share sent. Its bootstrap confidence interval excludes zero. The estimates for Self-interest and Cooperation are negative, but their confidence intervals include zero.

In grouped 10-fold cross-validation, adding the weights raises out-of-sample $R^2$ from 0.129 to 0.137 ($\Delta R^2=0.008$) and lowers RMSE from 0.2115 to 0.2105. Thus the weights add predictive information, but the incremental gain is small.
