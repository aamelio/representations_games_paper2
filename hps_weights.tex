\section{Between-subject analysis}

\subsection{Four-category method}

Each participant-frame (one participant under one set of instructions) provides four HP texts, one for each hypothetical allocation $a\in\{4,6,8,12\}$. Each text is classified as Moral (M), Self-interest (S), Cooperation (C), or No clear justification (N). We estimate a multinomial model with allocation-specific and participant-specific terms:

\begin{equation}
    \log\frac{\Pr(K_{ia}=k)}{\Pr(K_{ia}=N)}=\alpha_{ka}+b_{ki},
    \qquad k\in\{M,S,C\},
\end{equation}

where the participant-frame effects $b_{ki}$ are regularized toward zero. Cross-validation selects the strength of this regularization. The allocation effects $\alpha_{ka}$ absorb systematic differences across the four hypothetical allocations; the second stage uses the estimated $b_{Mi}$, $b_{Si}$, and $b_{Ci}$ directly. These effects measure participant deviations in category log odds relative to No clear justification, net of differences across the four hypothetical allocations.

We regress the participant's DG share sent on the three $b_{ki}$ effects, controlling for indicators for LT Control, KW Market, and LT Market, with KW Control omitted. Standard errors are clustered by participant. Confidence intervals refit both stages in 500 bootstrap samples drawn at the participant level, holding the regularization strength at its cross-validated value.

\subsection{Moral baseline, excluding No clear justification}

We remove all No clear justification classifications and re-estimate the model on M, S, and C, using Moral as the reference category:

\begin{equation}
    \log\frac{\Pr(K_{ia}=k)}{\Pr(K_{ia}=M)}=\alpha_{ka}+b_{ki},
    \qquad k\in\{S,C\}.
\end{equation}

The analysis retains 3,059 substantive HP classifications from 1,060 participant-frames. The 140 frames containing only No clear classifications are excluded. The estimated $b_{Si}$ and $b_{Ci}$ are participant-frame deviations in the log odds of Self-interest and Cooperation relative to Moral, net of differences across the four hypothetical allocations.

\subsection{Heterogeneity by game and condition}

This regression includes indicators for LT Control, KW Market, and LT Market, with KW Control omitted; hence average allocations may differ across all four game-by-condition cells. For the condition comparison, we retain only KW observations and interact the participant effects with a Market indicator, using KW Control as the reference cell.

\section{Within-subject analysis}

\subsection{Cell-specific HP weights}

The within-subject data contain 1,799 participants and 14,392 HP responses from two pre-choice elicitations. We apply the same M/S/C/N classification prompt, exclude N responses, and estimate separately for each game-by-condition cell $c$:

\begin{equation}
\log\frac{\Pr(K_{ia}=k\mid c)}{\Pr(K_{ia}=M\mid c)}=\alpha^c_{ka}+b^c_{ki},\qquad k\in\{S,C\}.
\end{equation}

The models use 9,467 HP responses classified as M, S, or C and retain 3,119 participant-by-treatment-cell observations. An observation is excluded when all four HP responses are classified N. Participant effects are regularized toward zero, with the strength selected separately by within-cell cross-validation. M/S/C weights are the fitted probabilities averaged over the four allocation anchors.

The KW--LT comparison combines a systematic downward shift in Moral weight under LT with limited persistence across games: 57.1\% of participants are in a lower tercile under LT, 11.3\% are in a higher tercile, and 31.6\% remain in the same tercile ($r=0.187$). The Control--Market contrast is substantially sharper: 76.6\% are in a lower tercile under Market, only 1.6\% are higher, and 21.8\% remain in the same tercile. The near-zero correlation ($r=-0.049$) provides little evidence that participants preserve their relative Moral-weight ranking between Control and Market. Thus, the treatment differences involve both shifts in average Moral weight and changes in participants' relative positions.

All within-subject results are descriptive, in-sample associations. The weights are estimated from the HP classifications rather than directly observed, and the treatment decomposition does not isolate a causal mechanism.
