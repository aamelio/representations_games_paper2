\section{Stage 1: similarity to vignette game structures}

In the first stage, each of the 12 game--frame contexts is compared jointly
with all 22 revised vignettes. The vignettes comprise 6 DG, 8 UG, and 8 TG
cases.
Because these families contain different numbers of vignettes, the comparison
uses the mean allocated points per vignette in family $g$ after averaging
vignette ratings across the three agents,

\begin{equation}
    \bar p_{cv}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{a=1}^{3}p_{acv},
    \qquad
    \mu_{cg}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_{g}|}
    \sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{g}}\bar p_{cv},
    \qquad
    s_{cg}=\frac{\mu_{cg}}{\sum_h\mu_{ch}}.
\end{equation}

Using family totals instead would mechanically favor the two larger vignette
families. We therefore normalize the three family means so that
$\sum_g s_{cg}=1$ within each target context. Figure~\ref{fig:structural-control}
reports these normalized shares in Control. The matching vignette family
receives 85.6 percent of the normalized similarity share for the DG target,
79.8 percent for the UG target, and 78.5 percent for the TG target. Thus, the
revised vignette set continues to distinguish the three underlying game
structures sharply.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{relative_structural_control.png}
    \caption{Family-size-adjusted structural similarity shares for the three
    Control contexts. Within each target game, the three bars are the
    normalized family means and sum to 100 percent. Error bars are 95 percent
    Student-$t$ intervals across the three agent-level estimates.}
    \label{fig:structural-control}
\end{figure}

\section{Stage 2: representations within game structure}

In the second stage, each context is rated in a new task containing only
vignettes constructed around the same game: 6 candidates for DG and 8 for UG
or TG. The 1,000-point budget is therefore reallocated separately within each
game-specific choice set. After rating, individual vignette points are summed
within representation class $k$ and expressed as a share,

\begin{equation}
    q_{ack}=\frac{1}{1{,}000}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{g,k}}p_{acv},
    \qquad
    \bar q_{ck}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{a=1}^{3}q_{ack},
    \qquad \sum_k \bar q_{ck}=1.
\end{equation}

DG has three sender classes: Moral (M), Self-interest (S), and Cooperation
(C). The revised UG set contains four joint-action classes: M-C, S-C, C-C,
and C-D. The revised TG set contains M-C, S-D, C-C, and C-D. In these labels,
the first component is the sender representation and the second is the
receiver action. Each class is represented by one personal and one anonymous-
market vignette. These classifications are used only after the similarity
ratings have been recorded.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{relative_within_game_control.png}
    \caption{Within-game relative similarity in Control. Bars give each
    representation class's share of the 1,000 points and sum to 100 percent
    within each target game. Error bars are 95 percent Student-$t$ intervals
    across the three agent-level shares.}
    \label{fig:within-control}
\end{figure}

In Control, the DG similarity budget is split between Moral (33.8 percent),
Self-interest (34.5 percent), and Cooperation (31.7 percent). The four revised
UG classes range from 24.2 to 25.7 percent, and the four revised TG classes
range from 24.0 to 26.5 percent. The Control instructions therefore do not
strongly privilege a single representation within the UG or TG choice sets.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{relative_within_game_market_minus_control.png}
    \caption{Change in within-game representation shares: Market minus
    Control. Differences sum to zero within each target game. Error bars are
    95 percent Student-$t$ intervals for the three paired agent-level
    differences.}
    \label{fig:within-market-control}
\end{figure}

Relative to Control, Market shifts 1.7 percentage points toward Cooperation
in DG (95 percent interval: 0.2 to 3.1) and 2.8 points away from Moral
($-4.7$ to $-0.9$). Before neutralizing the DG-C wording, the corresponding
Cooperation point estimate was 3.9 points. The attenuation is descriptively
consistent with the incidental wording having contributed to the magnitude
of the result, although the two rounds use different model draws and their
difference is not itself an estimated contrast. In the original UG ratings,
Market shifts 6.2 points away from M-C ($-10.9$ to $-1.5$); the positive
changes in C-C, S-C, and C-D have intervals that include zero. In the original
TG ratings, M-C falls by 1.7 points ($-2.4$ to $-0.9$), while the intervals for
the other three changes include zero.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{relative_within_game_aid_minus_bonus.png}
    \caption{Change in within-game representation shares: Aid minus Bonus.
    Differences sum to zero within each target game. Error bars are 95 percent
    Student-$t$ intervals for the three paired agent-level differences.}
    \label{fig:within-aid-bonus}
\end{figure}

Relative to Bonus, Aid shifts similarity toward Self-interest: S rises by
13.5 percentage points in DG (0.9 to 26.1). In the original UG ratings, S-C
rises by 15.7 points, but its wide interval includes zero; C-C falls by 7.2
points ($-12.2$ to $-2.1$). In the original TG ratings, all four Aid-minus-
Bonus intervals include zero. The wide intervals in several cells reflect the
small number of model runs.

\section{Similarity of hypothetical-allocation descriptions}

Each description was rated against
the DG-KW Control instructions and, in a separate blinded task, against the
DG-KW Market instructions. For each description--instruction pair, we first
average the three independent 0--100 similarity ratings. The figures report
response-level means of these three-rater averages. Because each participant
provided four hypothetical-allocation descriptions, 95 percent confidence
intervals are clustered by participant.

Figure~\ref{fig:hp_similarity_source_condition} compares descriptions from
each source condition with both instruction texts. Control descriptions are
more similar to the Control instructions than to the Market instructions
(42.3 versus 14.9). The ordering reverses for Market descriptions, which are
more similar to the Market instructions than to the Control instructions
(38.9 versus 13.3).

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{hp_similarity_by_source_condition.png}
    \caption{Mean similarity of hypothetical-allocation descriptions to the
    DG-KW Control and Market instructions, by the source condition of the
    description. Bar heights average the three-rater mean over HP responses;
    error bars are 95 percent confidence intervals clustered by participant.}
    \label{fig:hp_similarity_source_condition}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:hp_similarity_by_category} fixes the source condition and the
corresponding instruction text, then compares descriptions across the three
substantive classifications. Among Control descriptions, Moral descriptions
have the highest mean similarity to the Control instructions (48.6), followed
by Self-interest (40.2) and Mutual Benefit/Cooperation (22.4). Among Market
descriptions, Mutual Benefit/Cooperation descriptions have the highest mean
similarity to the Market instructions (56.6), while Moral and Self-interest
descriptions have similar means (36.2 and 35.4). The Control
Mutual Benefit/Cooperation cell is small (21 responses from 20 participants),
as reflected in its wider confidence interval.
