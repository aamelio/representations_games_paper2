# Joint-action vignette pilot: design and codebook

## Purpose

This pilot replaces the earlier category-by-belief vignette design with outcome
prototypes. Each vignette is a completed episode: it specifies the sender's
reason and action and, where the structure gives the receiver agency, the
receiver's subsequent action. The receiver action is an element of the outcome
prototype, not an expectation stated by the sender.

The intended interpretation is that a sender may perceive a decision as similar
to episodes with a particular joint-action outcome. Beliefs can then arise
endogenously from that perceived similarity. The vignettes themselves therefore
do not say what the sender expects the receiver to do.

## Pilot matrix

The pilot contains 30 conceptual cells and two settings per cell, for 60
vignettes in total.

| Structure | Sender category | Sender action | Receiver action | Cells |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DG | 3 | 2 | Not applicable | 6 |
| UG | 3 | 2 | 2 | 12 |
| TG | 3 | 2 | 2 | 12 |

Each cell has:

- one interaction involving individuals;
- one interaction involving a firm, entrepreneur, manager, or market exchange.

The individual vignettes are balanced between counterparts who know each other
and unfamiliar counterparts. The market vignettes vary between ongoing business
relationships and relatively anonymous or one-off transactions.

## Structural definitions

### DG

- The sender initially controls a fixed resource.
- The sender unilaterally determines its allocation.
- The total resource does not change as a result of the decision.
- There is no receiver action.
- Sender C allocates a meaningful part to the recipient.
- Sender D keeps all or nearly all of the resource.

### UG

- The sender proposes how to divide a fixed resource or surplus.
- The receiver subsequently accepts or rejects the proposal.
- Acceptance implements the proposed division.
- Rejection destroys the available surplus: both sides receive nothing or lose
  the transaction-specific benefit.
- Sender C makes a relatively balanced or accommodating proposal.
- Sender D makes a strongly sender-favoring proposal.
- Receiver C accepts; Receiver D rejects.

### TG

- The sender initially controls a resource and chooses how much to entrust.
- The entrusted amount creates or expands a surplus.
- The receiver then chooses how much of the expanded proceeds to return.
- Sender C entrusts a substantial amount.
- Sender D entrusts a low but strictly positive amount, preserving a genuine
  receiver choice.
- Receiver C returns a meaningful share; Receiver D retains almost all of the
  expanded proceeds.

## Sender categories

The sender category describes the reason made salient in the vignette, not the
objective character of the action.

- **Moral:** the sender appeals to fairness, duty, rights, promises, deservingness,
  or a rule about what ought to be done.
- **Self-interest:** the sender focuses on personal or organizational advantage,
  security, convenience, reputation, or avoiding a private cost.
- **Cooperation:** the sender focuses on maintaining a workable relationship,
  coordinating plans, or producing an outcome that both sides can use.

All three categories are crossed with both sender actions. Some combinations,
especially Moral-D and Cooperation-D, are intentionally demanding pilot cells.
They should be evaluated for naturalness before expanding to the final pool.

## Writing constraints

The vignette text must:

- make the initial control of resources clear;
- make the structural consequence of the receiver's decision clear in UG and TG;
- state the sender's concrete action and the reason for it;
- state the receiver's concrete action in UG and TG;
- describe realized actions rather than the sender's expectation;
- avoid analytical labels such as the category names, C/D, and game names;
- avoid identifying the intended cell through headings shown to the rater;
- avoid extra strategic actors whose choices change the core structure;
- remain concise and self-contained.

The CSV metadata are for researchers only and should not be shown to the
similarity rater.

## Planned similarity analysis

The absolute similarity scores should support two complementary summaries:

1. structural weights across DG-, UG-, and TG-like vignettes;
2. within-structure weights, renormalized over the vignettes belonging to the
   relevant structure, across sender categories and action profiles.

This separation prevents structural matching from mechanically obscuring the
within-game association between frames and joint-action prototypes.
