# Reduced joint-action vignette pool: design and codebook

## Purpose

This round replaces the 60-vignette factorial pilot with a smaller pool guided
by the allocations that are substantively salient in the experiment and by the
reason/action combinations observed in Control. It preserves the distinction
between DG-, UG-, and TG-structured situations, but no longer crosses every
reason category with both sender actions.

Each vignette is a completed outcome prototype. It states the sender's reason
and action and, in UG and TG, the receiver's subsequent action. It does not
state what the sender expected the receiver to do.

## Allocation anchors

The DG hypothetical-allocation exercise uses four allocations out of twelve,
reported here as `(sender, receiver)`:

- `(4, 8)`;
- `(6, 6)`;
- `(8, 4)`;
- `(12, 0)`.

These anchors provide a common vocabulary, but they are not forced into every
game when doing so would contradict the intended reason or strategic structure.

- **Moral:** a fair allocation, `(6, 6)` in DG and UG.
- **Self-interest under unilateral control:** the sender invokes the right to
  retain the resource and maximizes own payoff, `(12, 0)` in DG.
- **Self-interest with possible retaliation:** the sender retains most but gives
  enough to make acceptance attractive, `(8, 4)` in UG.
- **Cooperation:** the sender gives a substantial amount so that the high
  joint-return outcome can be realized, `(4, 8)` in UG.

TG starts from six units. The corresponding sender actions are:

- Moral: send 3, which can produce a fair final allocation if the receiver
  returns 3 of the 9 generated units;
- Self-interest: send 1, retaining 5 and exposing little of the endowment;
- Cooperation: send all 6, producing the largest available joint surplus.

This design omits a DG Cooperation cell because a high-return productive
transfer is not part of the DG structure. It also omits Moral-D and
Cooperation-D sender profiles.

## Settings

Half of the vignettes involve named individuals. The text simply identifies
the people involved and does not classify their relationship as familiar or
unfamiliar.

Half involve firms or market participants. Every market interaction is
explicitly anonymous and one-shot: the parties have no past relationship,
their identities are hidden or irrelevant, and there is no future business
between them.

## Structural requirements

### DG

The sender controls a resource and chooses its final allocation. The vignette
does not add explanations about a passive receiver or a fixed pie when these
facts are already apparent from the allocation.

### UG

The sender proposes a division of twelve units. The receiver accepts or
rejects. Acceptance implements the proposal; rejection gives both parties
nothing. Both receiver responses are represented for every sender profile.

### TG

The sender starts with six units. The amount sent is tripled. The receiver then
chooses how much to return. Both a meaningful return and no return are
represented for every sender profile.

## Writing constraints

The vignette prose must:

- use simple, comprehensible transactions;
- state all quantities and consequences needed to understand the interaction;
- make the sender's reason and concrete action clear;
- make the receiver's concrete action clear in UG and TG;
- avoid stating the sender's expectation;
- avoid analytical labels such as Moral, Self-interest, Cooperation, C/D, and
  the names of the experimental games;
- avoid unnecessary institutional or contractual detail;
- avoid ongoing or repeated relationships in market cases.

The CSV metadata are for researchers only and should not be shown to the
similarity rater or survey respondent.
