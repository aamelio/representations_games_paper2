## Class G (fair_minded)

**G1 - individual; proposed_terms.** Oskar, 29, spent a winter rebuilding a vintage motorcycle's engine while his friend Denny sourced the frame and rare parts. When the bike sells, Denny proposes taking the larger share to cover what the parts actually cost him. Oskar could argue the buyer came through his own contacts, but the receipts are real and Denny fronted them for months. He agrees to the split Denny deserves.

**G2 - individual; entrusted_proceeds.** Marisol, 61, sells jars of honey at the Saturday market for her neighbor Aldo, who is recovering from surgery and never counts the jars he leaves with her. This week everything sold at a premium to a caterer. Marisol sets aside every cent of Aldo's agreed portion, calculated on the higher price, before touching her own cut. The bees, the hives, the labor — all his.

**G3 - firm; proposed_terms.** Ingrid runs a neighborhood grocery. Her produce co-op proposes new wholesale terms: prices up eleven percent to reflect a drought season's real growing costs. A rival supplier is undercutting the co-op with imports, and switching would fatten Ingrid's margins. She signs the co-op's terms anyway. The farmers absorbed the bad year; the increase matches what they actually spent, and she won't pocket the difference their losses created.

**G4 - firm; entrusted_proceeds.** Dmitri owns a small gallery that sells ceramics on consignment. A potter, Lena, left him a crate of pieces priced modestly; a collector bought the lot well above the listed figures, and Lena has no way of knowing. Dmitri writes her check as a percentage of the actual sale, not the list price. The work commanded that money because she made it that good.


## Class B (indignant)

**B1 - individual; proposed_terms.** Yara, 45, needs to sell her late husband's boat quickly. Her brother-in-law Piet, who knows the marina crowd, offers to broker the sale — keeping ninety percent for his connections. Even a tenth would exceed what Yara could get alone before the storage fees hit. She refuses outright and lets the deadline pass, telling Piet she would rather absorb the loss than reward someone squeezing a widow.

**B2 - individual; entrusted_proceeds.** Odile, 41, spent two months selling her uncle Gustav's stamp albums at collectors' fairs, splitting proceeds evenly after he handed the collection over. Settling up, a dealer shows her the albums were already declared stolen on Gustav's insurance claim — she had been moving them so his settlement would stand, her name on every receipt. Odile turns the albums and every cent over to the insurer, keeps nothing, and tells Gustav he chose the wrong cover.

**B3 - firm; proposed_terms.** Petra owns a corner café. A national distributor offers her a supply contract: exclusivity, prices adjustable at their sole discretion, penalties if she stocks anything local. The numbers would still beat her current costs. Petra tears up the offer at the sales rep's table and emails every café owner she knows describing the clauses, accepting higher costs herself to make the terms publicly radioactive.

**B4 - firm; entrusted_proceeds.** Silas stocks his delicatessen with estate olive oil fronted by importer Corbin, selling at Corbin's set price and remitting proceeds minus a fifth. Midway through the season, Silas finds Corbin's website selling identical bottles at nearly half that price: his shop was the overpriced front making Corbin's 'direct discount' look like a bargain. Incensed at being used as a prop, Silas keeps the season's proceeds as payment for the charade and tells Corbin to sue if he likes.


## Class S (own_payoff)

**S1 - individual; proposed_terms.** Wendy, 38, stored her sister Carol's inherited paintings in her garage for years. Carol has found a buyer and proposes the split: Carol keeps nine-tenths, Wendy gets the rest for storage. Wendy runs one calculation — the small share versus the empty garage and nothing. The share wins. How the rest compares to her trouble never enters it; she signs and asks when the money clears.

**S2 - individual; entrusted_proceeds.** Bram, 26, took a friend's advance to buy rare comics at a convention, with a loose understanding that profits would flow back and be shared. Resale went brilliantly. Nothing was written down, the friend lives abroad, and no consequence follows from silence. Bram tallies what he can retain without repercussion — all of it — and moves the full amount into his own savings.

**S3 - firm; proposed_terms.** Aiko owns a print shop. A hotel chain proposes a year-long printing contract at rates below her usual list: menus, signage, stationery on a fixed monthly schedule. She runs the numbers once — the guaranteed volume fills her presses' idle hours and yields a modest but reliable margin. That settles it; she signs. Whether the chain's savings dwarf her profit is not a column in her spreadsheet.

**S4 - firm; entrusted_proceeds.** Lorcan runs an auction house. An estate executor consigned furniture under a written agreement: sale at auction, a standard commission to Lorcan, the balance remitted within thirty days. The pieces sell well. Lorcan deducts his full contracted commission — the same rate he charges every consignor — and wires the remainder on schedule. Whether the executor considers the proceeds ample or thin plays no part in his accounting; his rate covers costs and margin.


## Class C (partnership)

**C1 - individual; proposed_terms.** Sylvie, 57, grows vegetables beside her neighbor Marco's plot. Marco proposes pooling: his tiller and irrigation line, her greenhouse, with him taking a somewhat larger share of this year's harvest since his equipment does the heavy work. Sylvie agrees on the spot. Together the plots will yield far more than either alone, and next season's expanded beds — the reason she's in this — need the arrangement to hold.

**C2 - individual; entrusted_proceeds.** Priya, 33, launched a market stall with startup cash her friend Devi advanced, no strings attached. The first quarter beat every projection. Priya could quietly bank the surplus, but instead she repays Devi's advance with a share of the profits on top and a proposal: fund a second stall together. What Priya wants is the growing operation both of them can build, not one good season.

**C3 - firm; proposed_terms.** Tobias owns a small brewery. A hop farm up the valley proposes terms: prices slightly above the commodity market, locked for two years, in exchange for first pick of each harvest and joint planning of new varieties. Cheaper hops exist by the container. Tobias signs anyway — a farm that thrives will experiment with him, and the beers they develop together are worth more than the spread he'd save.

**C4 - firm; entrusted_proceeds.** Colette's design studio received a large advance from a longtime client for a rebranding project. The work wraps under budget, leaving a surplus in the studio's account that the client hasn't asked about. Colette credits the entire remainder toward the project's next phase and sends a proposal for expanded packaging work. A client who keeps commissioning is where the real value lies, and she prices accordingly.
