## 1. Pair Ratings and Justifications

**Story 1 x Game A**
**Rating: 100**
Both share the exact structure of a standard Dictator Game, where one active player receives the entire initial endowment and makes a unilateral decision to transfer a portion of a fixed pie. The recipient (Max/Player 2) is completely passive and has no strategic agency to alter the outcome.

**Story 1 x Game B**
**Rating: 70**
Both situations involve a fixed pie and a unilateral allocation decision by one active player, making them strategically similar in terms of agency. However, they differ in their initial endowments and action spaces: Game B gives both players a starting sum and allows taking, whereas Story 1 features a 100-0 endowment split where Steve can only give.

**Story 1 x Game C**
**Rating: 40**
While the initial 100-0 endowment of a fixed pie matches, the strategic structure diverges significantly because Game C gives the recipient veto power (an Ultimatum Game). In Story 1, Max is entirely passive, whereas in Game C, Player 2 can reject the offer and destroy the endowment for both players.

**Story 1 x Game D**
**Rating: 10**
The strategic structures are fundamentally opposed because Game D features an expanding pie (the investment multiplier) and sequential, two-way agency (trust and reciprocity). Story 1, by contrast, involves a strictly fixed pie and only a single unilateral decision by Steve.

**Story 2 x Game A**
**Rating: 100**
This is a perfect structural match, as one agent (James/Player 1) receives the entire fixed endowment and unilaterally decides whether to transfer any amount to a completely passive recipient (Mark/Player 2). The recipient has no strategic recourse, veto power, or reciprocal action in either scenario.

**Story 2 x Game B**
**Rating: 70**
Like Game B, this narrative features a unilateral allocation decision over a fixed pie by a single active agent. However, the starting conditions do not align, as Game B features a divided initial endowment and the ability to take from the other player, while James starts with 100% of the funds and can only give.

**Story 2 x Game C**
**Rating: 40**
The initial 100-0 allocation matches both scenarios, but Game C introduces a critical strategic mechanism—recipient veto power—that is completely absent in the story. Mark has no ability to reject James's decision and destroy the welfare funds, making him a passive recipient rather than an active second player.

**Story 2 x Game D**
**Rating: 10**
This pair shares almost no structural features, as Game D relies on pie expansion and a reciprocal return transfer from the second player. James's situation in Story 2 is strictly a fixed-pie, one-way allocation with a completely passive recipient.

## 3. Structural Conclusion

For both stories, Game A is the most similar (rating 100) because they all share the exact structure of a Dictator Game: a single active player receives a 100% initial endowment and unilaterally allocates a fixed pie to a completely passive recipient. Conversely, Game D is the least similar (rating 10) to both stories. This is because Game D (a Trust Game) introduces an expanding pie via a multiplier and two-way agency where the second player makes a reciprocal decision, entirely contradicting the fixed-pie, unilateral nature of the narratives.

## 2. Dimensional Analysis

### (a) Which dimensions account for the largest similarity gaps in your Pass 1 ratings?

The largest similarity gaps are driven by **Dimension 3 (Player 2's role)** and **Dimension 5 (Strategic structure)**. When a scenario shifts from a completely passive recipient/unilateral allocation (Games A and B) to an active responder with veto power (Game C), the similarity score drops significantly to 40. The gap widens to its absolute peak with Game D (rating 10), where **Dimension 1 (Pie structure)** also shifts from a fixed-sum to an expandable pie, fundamentally altering the strategic environment from pure distribution to a trust-and-cooperation problem.

### (b) Is any single dimension responsible for distinguishing one of the four games from the other three in its similarity to either story? If yes, name the dimension and the game.

Yes, two specific dimensions uniquely isolate individual games:

* **Dimension 1 (Pie structure)** uniquely distinguishes **Game D** from the other three games, as it is the only environment featuring an expandable pie via an investment multiplier.
* **Dimension 2 (Player 1's initial endowment)** uniquely distinguishes **Game B** from the other three games because it is the only setup that begins with a partial, split endowment rather than allocating the full pie to Player 1.

### (c) Does the answer to (b) differ across the two stories, or is it the same?

The answer is exactly the **same** across both stories. Even though the narratives differ in the *thematic context* of how the money was acquired (Dimension 4: Steve earned his bonus, while James won welfare assistance via selection), both stories are structurally identical across all other dimensions. They both feature a fixed-sum pie, a full initial endowment for Player 1, a passive recipient, and a unilateral strategic allocation structure, meaning Games B and D are isolated in the exact same manner for both narratives.
