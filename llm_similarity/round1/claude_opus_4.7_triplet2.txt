**Game A × Story 1: 10**
The trust game's defining features are pie expansion via a multiplier and sequential reciprocity, with both players holding decision power. In Story 1, Mark is completely passive, there is no multiplier, and James faces a one-shot allocation choice. The only commonality is asymmetric initial endowments.

**Game A × Story 2: 10**
Same structural mismatch: no multiplier, no return move, Max has no decision to make. The collaborative framing (Max contributed to earning the bonus) is thematically suggestive but does not introduce the trust game's mechanics — the pie is already determined when Steve chooses.

**Game B × Story 1: 8**
The ultimatum game is defined by Player 2's veto power, which turns the outcome into a joint product of both choices. Mark has no opportunity to accept or reject; he is not even portrayed as a counterparty in the decision. Without the veto, the strategic core of the ultimatum game is absent.

**Game B × Story 2: 8**
Same reasoning. Max is not described as having any acceptance/rejection role; the director hands the choice to Steve unilaterally and Max's preferences do not enter the payoff structure. Fixed pie is the only shared feature.

**Game C × Story 1: 30**
Game C and Story 1 share the feature of a single-sided allocation decision over a fixed pie, with the recipient passive. But Game C's distinguishing element is that the counterparty starts with a positive endowment that can be taken, whereas Mark starts at zero — the "take" branch of Game C's strategy space is unavailable. The choice set in the story is a strict subset of Game C's, and coincides with Game D's.

**Game C × Story 2: 28**
Same logic. Max has nothing to take from, so Steve's effective choice set is [0, full bonus], matching Game D rather than Game C. The unequal-but-positive endowments that motivate Game C's design are absent.

**Game D × Story 1: 92**
Direct structural match: one party is endowed with the full amount, the other with nothing, the recipient is passive, the pie is fixed, and the decider chooses any transfer in [0, full]. The welfare-selection framing also resembles Game D's windfall endowment from an external source. Minor residual difference: the story carries social context (acquaintance, need) absent in the lab description, but the choice architecture is identical.

**Game D × Story 2: 86**
Same dictator structure: full endowment with Steve, Max passive, fixed pie, unilateral transfer choice in [0, full]. The deduction relative to Story 1 reflects that the bonus is partly attributable to Max's joint effort, which complicates the "windfall" character of Game D's endowment — structurally the choice is still a dictator allocation, but the moral entitlement of the recipient is more salient than in a pure windfall.

=== PROMPT 3 ANSWER ===
**Game A (Trust): Story 1 / Story 2 = 25 / 75**
The trust game's distinctive cue is the multiplier — value grows through interaction — which a participant maps onto situations involving joint value creation, business cooperation, or workplace trust. Story 2 carries a workplace and team-effort frame that cues these associations even though the structural match is weak. Story 1 has no analogous joint-value-creation surface to grip onto.

**Game B (Ultimatum): Story 1 / Story 2 = 35 / 65**
The ultimatum game cues bargaining and take-it-or-leave-it dynamics — most commonly retrieved via salary negotiations, business deals, or workplace splits where the counterparty has implicit power to walk away or retaliate. Story 2's workplace context is closer to that retrieval cluster, since not sharing with Max plausibly damages future cooperation. Story 1's neighbor relation carries weaker implicit veto power.

**Game C (Take-or-give): Story 1 / Story 2 = 40 / 60**
Game C's framing of a joint pre-allocation ("you and your match are provisionally allocated… adding to a total of $12") cues team and shared-resource situations, where two parties have a stake in a common pool. Story 2's team-bonus context fits this template more naturally than Story 1's individual welfare allocation. Neither story matches structurally on the partial-endowment dimension, so retrieval is driven by surface theme.

**Game D (Dictator): Story 1 / Story 2 = 60 / 40**
Game D's "provisional allocation" framing is purely windfall — no work, no source, just an external assignment of full pie to one party. Story 1's selection-by-welfare-agency is a clean surface match to that windfall character. Story 2's bonus is earned through joint effort, which adds a contribution/entitlement frame that the dictator game's instructions do not evoke.

**Pass 1 vs. retrieval direction.** The two passes diverge sharply, and in an informative way. In the retrieval direction, Story 2 dominates for three of the four games (A, B, C) — even though it is no closer structurally — because its workplace/team surface evokes the cooperation, negotiation, and shared-stake themes that those games activate. This is the classic surface-vs-structure asymmetry in analogical retrieval: retrieval is driven by thematic cues, while structural similarity judgments are not. The only game where structural and retrieval rankings align is D, where Story 1's pure-windfall framing happens to match both the structural and surface cues of the dictator game.
