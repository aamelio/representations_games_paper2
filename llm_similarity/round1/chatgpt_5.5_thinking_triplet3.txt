## Pair ratings and justifications

### Game A × Story 1: **60/100**

Both involve a fixed pie and a unilateral decision by one person about whether to improve the other person’s allocation. However, Game A starts with both players already having money and allows the decision-maker to take from the other player, whereas Story 1 starts with James receiving the full aid amount and Mark receiving nothing.

### Game A × Story 2: **58/100**

The story and game both involve a passive recipient and a one-sided redistribution choice over a fixed amount. The mismatch is that Game A begins with a split endowment and permits taking from the other participant, while Steve begins with the full bonus and can only decide whether to give some away.

### Game B × Story 1: **94/100**

This is very close structurally: James has the entire endowment, Mark has none, and James can choose whether to transfer some amount to Mark. The recipient is passive, the pie is fixed, and the outcome depends only on James’s allocation decision.

### Game B × Story 2: **96/100**

This is almost the same strategic situation: Steve receives the full bonus and may give any portion to Max, with the final decision entirely his. The main extra feature in the story is that Max contributed to producing the bonus, which affects moral interpretation but not the immediate strategic structure.

### Game C × Story 1: **15/100**

Game C is a trust game: the first player’s transfer expands the pie, and the second player then chooses whether to return some money. Story 1 lacks both the multiplier and the second player’s response, so it is much closer to a passive-recipient allocation problem.

### Game C × Story 2: **24/100**

Story 2 has some background resemblance to joint production because Steve and Max worked together to earn the bonus. But the actual decision is not a trust interaction: Steve’s transfer does not multiply, and Max has no subsequent choice to reciprocate.

### Game D × Story 1: **50/100**

Both involve one person initially controlling the full amount and deciding how much to offer the other person. The key mismatch is that in Game D the recipient can accept or reject the offer, potentially reducing both payoffs to zero, while Mark has no such veto in Story 1.

### Game D × Story 2: **49/100**

Game D matches the full initial allocation and transfer proposal structure. But it gives Player 2 strategic agency through rejection, whereas Story 2 explicitly says the final decision is Steve’s alone.

### (a) Which dimensions account for the largest similarity gaps?

The largest gaps come from **Player 2’s role** and **strategic structure**. ### (b) Is any single dimension responsible for distinguishing one game from the other three?

Yes. **Pie structure** most clearly distinguishes **Game C** from the other three games, because it is the only one where the pie can expand through Player 1’s action. Closely related, **Player 2’s role** also distinguishes Game C because Player 2 is a reciprocator with a continuous choice rather than a passive recipient or veto player.
